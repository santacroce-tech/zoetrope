Timeline and tweens

The timeline is where things change over time. Each row is a layer; each cell is a frame; a filled dot is a keyframe.

Layers

  • ＋ Layer adds a layer above the active one; ＋ Folder groups layers; 🗑 deletes the active layer.
  • Double-click a name to rename it. Drag rows to reorder them or drop them into folders. Higher rows draw on top.
  • The eye hides a layer; the dot locks it so you can't select its objects by accident.
  • Guide turns a layer into a guide layer. It's visible while editing, for sketches and layout lines, but is never exported.

Frames and keyframes

A keyframe is where a layer's content changes. Between keyframes, the layer holds or tweens its content. Click a cell to select that frame, then:

CommandKeyEffect
Insert frameF5Lengthens the span (or extends the layer to this frame).
Remove frameShift F5Shortens the span.
KeyframeF6New keyframe here with a copy of the current content, ready to pose.
Blank keyframeF7New empty keyframe.
Clear keyframeShift F6Merges the keyframe into the previous span.

Play with Enter, step with , and ., jump with Home and End. The frame rate is set in the stage properties.

Tweens

Select a frame inside a span that is followed by another keyframe, then click Motion tween or Shape tween (or choose one under Tween in Properties).

  • Motion tweens interpolate position, scale, rotation, skew, pivot, opacity and tint. They work on anything, and are the usual choice for symbols. Spins adds full turns.
  • Shape tweens also morph shape geometry, fills and strokes. Rectangles tween to rectangles parametrically; different shapes are converted to paths and morphed point by point.
  • Objects are paired across the two keyframes by identity: a keyframe made with F6 copies its objects, so they pair up automatically.

Easing

Under Easing choose Linear, a preset (In, Out and In Out variants of Quad, Cubic, Sine, Back, Bounce and Elastic), or Custom (bezier) and drag the two handles. The curve plots progress over the span.

Onion skin

Onion shows faded neighbouring frames: earlier in blue, later in green. The two numbers set how many on each side. Locked layers aren't onion-skinned, which is handy for backgrounds.

Frame labels and scripts

In the frame's Label & script section you can name a keyframe (shown as ⚑name in the timeline) and attach a script (shown as a). Scripts jump to labels with gotoAndPlay("name"). See Scripting.
